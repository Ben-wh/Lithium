Welcome! This guide explains how to get help quickly. It's also an example of what the knowledge base can do, so staff can open it in the editor to see how it's written.

## Before you raise a ticket

- **Search this knowledge base first.** Common questions often already have an answer.
- **Check whether it's just you.** If colleagues have the same problem, mention it: it helps the team spot bigger issues.
- **Restart the app or device.** It fixes more than you'd think.

## Writing a good ticket

A clear ticket gets solved faster. Try to include:

1. **A short, specific subject.** "Can't print from Outlook on the 3rd floor" beats "Printer broken".
2. **What happened**, and what you expected to happen instead.
3. **When it started**, and anything that changed around then (an update, a new device, a move).
4. **Screenshots or photos**. You can attach up to five images when raising a ticket, or paste a screenshot straight in.
5. **The exact error message**, if there is one. Copy it as text so it can be searched.

> **Tip:** pick the category that fits best. It sends your ticket to the right people. If nothing fits, leave it blank and the team will sort it.

## Choosing a priority

| Priority | Use it when… |
| --- | --- |
| **Low** | It's minor and there's no rush |
| **Normal** | It needs doing but isn't stopping your work |
| **High** | It's blocking some of your work |
| **Urgent** | Something critical is down for you or others |

Please keep **Urgent** for real emergencies, so they stand out.

## After you've raised it

You'll see your ticket under **Tickets**, and you'll get a notification (the bell at the top) when someone replies or the status changes. Ticket statuses mean:

- **Unassigned**: waiting for someone to pick it up.
- **In progress**: someone's working on it.
- **Pending**: waiting on you or someone else. Check for a reply.
- **Resolved** or **Closed**: done. Reply if it isn't fixed.

## Checklist

- [x] Searched the knowledge base
- [ ] Wrote a specific subject
- [ ] Added screenshots and the error message
- [ ] Chose a category and priority

### For staff: editing articles

Articles are written in Markdown. Use the toolbar, or type formatting directly:

```
## A heading
**bold**, _italic_, `code` and [links](https://example.com)
- a bulleted list
1. a numbered list
> a highlighted tip
```

You can drag, paste or upload images into any article, choose who can read it (**Staff only** or **Everyone**), and keep it as a draft until it's ready.
